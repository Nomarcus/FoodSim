How it works

Two teams, blue and orange, try to volley the ball over the net. Max three touches per side, like real volleyball.

The evolved brain decides four things per player, 60 times a second: move, jump, hit angle and hit power. It sees the ball, where the ball will come down, its teammate and the net, all mirrored so every player thinks it plays from the left.

Ordinary code animates the body: spine, head, three-part arms (upper arm, forearm, hand) and three-part legs (thigh, shin, foot) are posed with inverse kinematics so the hands reach for the ball. Collisions use the forearms, hands, shins, feet, torso and head.

Every genome in a generation receives the same set of serves, so their scores are directly comparable.
