How it works

Two teams, blue and orange, try to volley the ball over the net. Max three touches per side, like real volleyball.

The evolved brain decides seven things per player, 60 times a second: move forward/back, move sideways, jump, hit angle, sideways aim, hit power and whether to throw a backflip in the air. It sees the ball, where the ball will come down, its teammate and the net, all mirrored so every player thinks it plays from the left.

Ordinary code animates the body: spine, head, three-part arms (upper arm, forearm, hand) and three-part legs (thigh, shin, foot) are posed with inverse kinematics so the hands reach for the ball. Hands and forearms strike, shins and feet kick (upside down in a flip that is a bicycle kick), and every other part only deflects the ball. Each part is drawn exactly as wide as its collision shape – tick “Show hitboxes” to see them.

Every genome in a generation receives the same set of serves, so their scores are directly comparable. Training always caps a serve at 25 s; ∞ Endless rally only lets the champion on show keep going for as long as the ball stays up.

The court is 3D: players move forwards, backwards and sideways, each has a home spot so the team covers the whole half, and a ball landing outside the side lines is out. Every brain starts with one instinct – head for your job: the ball if you are nearest to where it comes down, otherwise your spot – and evolution refines the rest. The 2D view is a side-on look at the same court. Drag the 3D view to orbit, scroll to zoom.
