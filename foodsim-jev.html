What the model decides. An agent that needs a new course reports what it can see: how far it can travel in each of eight directions before hitting an obstacle or the edge, and roughly where its goal lies — a fruit, or home once it is carrying one. Jev picks the direction. That single choice is the pathing and the obstacle avoidance both; there is no steering code underneath quietly correcting it.

What the code does. It moves the agent along the chosen heading, refuses to let it pass through an obstacle, and asks again when the agent arrives, gets blocked, or has travelled far enough that the view has changed. It also measures the sight lines that go into the question. None of it chooses a direction.

Why it looks different from the original. FoodSim Arena runs an evolved neural network for every agent on every frame, sixty times a second, and gets better over generations. This runs at the speed of a round trip and never improves — it has nothing to train. Every agent that needs a decision in the same tick is asked in one request, in parallel, which is why the fleet moves together rather than one at a time.
